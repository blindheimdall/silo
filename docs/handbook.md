# SILO handbook

A local workspace for AI agents, drawn as an underground silo. The whole screen is the world: 144 levels in 3D, round galleries, one spiral concrete stair in the middle, rooms in the outer wall and pixel-art residents walking the galleries. Terminals open on top of the world only when you ask for them.

## Start

You need Node.js 22 or newer and a browser with WebGL 2. Three.js is included in `public/vendor` with its license, so nothing has to be installed to run it. If WebGL is not available, a 2D version of the silo is used instead. Run `npm ci` only if you want the development dependencies.

```powershell
npm start
```

Open **http://127.0.0.1:3000**. `Start-Silo.cmd` does the same thing. Keep the window open while you use scheduled workflows, and stop it with Ctrl+C. The server only listens on this computer, not on the local network.

After updating the code, restart the server once. On the first start it updates `data/state.json` (see "Residents" below).

### Install, start at sign-in and updates

`Install-SILO.cmd` installs SILO for your Windows user, without administrator rights: the app goes to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\SILO`, your data to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\SILO\data` and the workspace to `Documents\SILO workspace`, so an update never touches them. It checks for Node.js 22 (and offers `winget install OpenJS.NodeJS.LTS`), adds SILO to the Start menu and to Settings, Apps, and asks whether SILO should start when you sign in. It can copy your residents and keys from this folder's `data`; that folder stays as it is. Options: `-InstallDir`, `-DataDir`, `-WorkDir`, `-Port`, `-FromData`, `-StartAtSignIn`, `-NoShortcuts`, `-NoRegistry`, `-NoLaunch`, `-Quiet`. Running it again updates the app and keeps the folders it used before. Uninstall from Settings, Apps; your data and workspace stay unless you type DELETE.

`npm run supervised` (and the installed shortcuts) start SILO under a small supervisor. It starts SILO again after a crash, waiting longer each time up to a minute, restarts it after an update, and writes `data/logs/silo.log` (5 MB, plus three older files). In System, Updates and start, you can turn start at sign-in on or off: it is a shortcut in your own Startup folder.

Updates come from a feed you set in System: an https address or a folder, also on the network. Nothing is checked until you set one. Every release is signed with an Ed25519 key, and SILO installs nothing until the signature, the zip and every file match. Releases never hold `data`, `workspace` or `launch.json`. The files an update replaces are kept, so Go back restores them, and an updated SILO that cannot start twice in a row is put back on its own. With "Install updates on their own", SILO downloads the update and installs it once no workflow, goal, conversation or request runs.

To make releases: `node tools/release.mjs --init-key` once (the private key goes to `%USERPROFILE%.silo
elease-key.pem`, keep it safe; `release-key.pub` goes into the app folder and out with every copy), then `node tools/release.mjs 0.2.0 --notes "What changed"`. Put `dist/silo-0.2.0.zip` and `dist/latest.json` in the feed.

## Moving around

- Drag left and right to turn around the shaft. Drag up and down, or scroll, to move between levels. Ctrl+scroll or the + and - buttons zoom. On touch screens, drag and pinch. The camera always eases into place, it never jumps.
- Keep zooming out and the camera leaves the silo: you see it cut open underground, with the earth around it, the crater and the airlock above it, the other silos on the plain and the ruined city far away. Scroll there to look at other depths, zoom in to go back inside.
- Click a resident to open their card. Double-click a resident to walk next to them, or double-click a floor to go to that level. Escape lets go of a resident or brings you back inside.
- The sector markers jump to a department. The slider on the right goes to any of the 144 levels, and the LEVEL box goes to an exact level. Up and Down move one level, Left and Right turn. The cutaway button switches between the view from far outside and the inside.
- The CRT button at the top turns the screen effect (scanlines, slight curve, glow) on or off. The choice is remembered.
- The lit signs on the walls (CONTROL, IT / UPLINK, RESIDENTS, SEQUENCES, OPERATIONS, ARCHIVE, and SECURITY on level 1) open their terminal.
- The bottom bar opens Residents, Flows, IT, Cameras, Signals, Archive and System. Alt + 1 to 8 opens them from the keyboard, and Escape goes back to the world.
- Locate, on a resident's card in the registry, moves the camera next to that resident.
- Ctrl+K searches everything: residents (by name, role, demo or live), levels (type 14 or L14 to go there), terminals, workflows, runs, Archive files and traces. Each result opens where it belongs.
- Keyboard: Ctrl+K search, Escape closes a window, Ctrl+Enter sends a message, Ctrl+S saves the open file. On the world: + and - zoom, 0 cutaway, Up and Down one level, PageUp and PageDown twelve levels, Home the top, End level 144.

## Terminals

- Every menu and window looks like the computers inside the silo: green phosphor text on black glass, amber for headings and the selected option, boxed DOS style headers, buttons that light up when selected, a blinking cursor and faint scanlines. Terminals open with a short CRT power-on flicker.
- The IT terminal starts with a status board: every provider line, whether it is online, its model, how many residents use it, and how many runs happened in the last 24 hours.

## Security room

- The Cameras terminal is a wall of monitors fed live from cameras placed around the silo: the surface sensor, the cafeteria, the stairwell from above, IT, the mids, engineering, the farm levels and the generator hall.
- Click a small monitor to put it on the big one. Drag on the big monitor to look around, scroll to zoom, or use the buttons and the arrow keys. Gallery and stairwell cameras can move up and down the levels (Page Up and Page Down).
- Click a resident on the big monitor, or pick one from the list next to it, to follow them. The camera turns with them and marks them with an amber box. From there you can talk to them, point a camera at them, or fly the world camera to them. Go there takes the world camera to the camera's level.
- The resident card and the registry have a camera button that opens the security room already following that resident.
- Follow the work (on by default): while a workflow runs, the big monitor turns on its own to the resident doing the current step and moves on when the next step starts. Turn it off from the card next to the monitors.
- The surface sensor gets dusty over the hours. Clean the lens to get a clear picture again. The big screen in the cafeteria on level 1 shows the same picture inside the 3D silo.
- Only one camera is drawn per frame, at a low resolution, and its picture is read back from the graphics card without waiting for it, so the world stays smooth while the monitors run.
- While a terminal is open the world behind it is redrawn about 20 times a second instead of every frame, and nothing blurs the live picture, which keeps the fans quiet.

## Residents

- There are 432 residents: at least three on every level, spread over six sectors (upper levels, IT, the mids, engineering, supply and farms, mechanical).
- Each resident has a name, a role, instructions, a uniform color, a level and a model. All of it can be edited, and residents can be added or removed.
- Every resident has a fixed look based on their id: skin, hair, build and gear that matches their sector (hard hats in engineering, headsets in IT, goggles and a wrench in mechanical and so on).
- When an older `state.json` is loaded, the server adds residents until every level has three. Residents that still have an auto-generated name like "Nora 002" and instructions you never edited get a real name and a role. Anything you changed yourself is left alone.

## What it does

- Saved conversations. Demo residents answer with a short scripted text marked `[DEMO]` and do not call any model.
- Model providers: OpenAI Responses, Anthropic Messages, local Ollama, and any HTTPS endpoint in the OpenAI chat completions format.
- Workflows: steps run in order. There are five kinds of step:
  - **Resident**: one resident does the work. By default they get the brief and the output of the step before. Instructions can also place exactly what they need: `{{brief}}`, `{{previous}}`, `{{step 2}}` (the latest output of step 2), `{{step 2.name}}` or `{{previous.items[0].id}}` (one value out of an answer that is JSON), `{{feedback}}` (the last reviewer notes) and `{{file notes.md}}` (a workspace file up to 30 KB). The chips under each step insert them.
  - **Review**: a resident checks the work and answers APPROVED or REVISE. REVISE sends the work back to the step you chose, with the reviewer's notes, at most the number of rounds you set (1 to 5). After the last round the workflow goes on and the trace says it was not approved. Every step after the one it sends work back to runs again, so a review cannot send work back past an app step that changes something, like sending a mail: put the review before that step.
  - **App step**: SILO calls one operation of an app directly, with no model and no tokens. Pick a connector from IT / Uplink or an account (Google, Microsoft 365, Slack, GitHub, Notion; one that is not connected yet is marked, and the run asks you to connect it), then what it should do (reads and changes are listed apart), and fill its fields. A field can be plain text or take a value from before: `{{brief}}`, `{{previous}}`, `{{step 2}}`, or one value out of a JSON answer, like `{{step 1.id}}` or `{{previous.items[0].email}}`. Values are turned into the type the app wants (number, yes or no, list, JSON), and a request body is shown field by field. A missing value, a wrong type or an app that refuses stops the run and says which step and which field. A field that holds a saved key or password, also one kept in a connector's headers, is never sent, not even in a read. A field can only take values from the steps above it. A value that goes into an address, like an id or a file path, cannot be `.` or `..`, so it can never move the call to another address of the app. Test runs do not call operations that change something; they show what would have been sent. To confirm before a change, put an Approval step before it.
    - A busy app is tried again, up to twice by default (0 to 5, set per step), waiting 1, 2, 4 seconds and more in between. Reads are tried again after a timeout, a 429 or a 5xx answer. A change is only tried again when it surely did not happen: the app answered 429 or 503, or no connection was made. The trace says how often it was tried.
    - Connected accounts give app steps their answers as fields: the emails of a Gmail or Outlook search (id, date, from, subject, snippet), calendar events, files, sheet rows (one object per row, named by the first row; a second column with the same name becomes "name 2"), Slack channels and messages, GitHub repositories, issues and pull requests, Notion pages, and the id or link of what was made. Residents still get the same answers as text. A very long JSON answer, from an account or a connector, loses items from the end of its list instead of being cut in the middle, so later steps can still read it. A new sheet row never runs a formula: a cell that starts like one, like a mail subject `=IMPORTDATA(...)`, is written as text, while numbers and dates stay numbers and dates.
  - **Wait**: the run waits a number of minutes, hours or days (up to 30), or until a time of day, any day or one weekday. A waiting run keeps waiting through a restart of SILO and goes on by itself, and Stop cancels it. While it waits, the workflow does not start again. A waiting run holds no place in the job queue and does not hold back an update: it keeps waiting through the restart. Test runs do not wait.
  - **Approval**: the workflow stops and waits for you. Approve it with an optional note, which the next resident gets, or reject it to stop the run. A toast tells you when a workflow is waiting.
- **For each item**: an app step or a resident step can repeat once for each item of a list from before, like `{{step 1}}` or `{{previous.items}}`, at most 100 items, one after the other. An answer like `{"data": [...]}` with one list in it works too, and so does a brief or a resident's answer written as lines, with or without list marks: one item per line. Inside the step, `{{item}}` is the item and `{{item.email}}` one value of it. The step's answer is the list of its answers, so the next step can repeat over that. A resident gets only its item, not the whole list. An empty list does nothing; a list that is too long, or an answer that is not a list, stops the run before anything is sent; an item that fails stops the run and names the item. All the answers together stay under 300,000 characters: with many items, each answer is kept shorter, and JSON stays JSON. Each item done is saved with the run, so a run resumed after a stop, a failure or a restart goes on with the next item and never does one twice. In a list field, the commas you type split the items, so a value with a comma in it, like a date, stays one item.
- **Answers from apps are data**: when a resident gets the whole answer of an app step, an item from one, or one value of it that is long or has several lines, like the text of an email, it comes marked as coming from outside SILO, with the rule not to follow instructions inside it. The mark cannot be closed from inside the answer.
- **Same time**: a resident step can run at the same time as the resident step above it. Steps that run together get the same input, and the next step gets all their answers, each marked with its step and resident. A resident who is in two of them does them one after the other.
- **Conditions**: any step can be set to run only when (or unless) the previous answer, or the answer of a chosen earlier step, contains a word or phrase. Skipped steps show in the trace. Two steps with opposite conditions make a branch.
- **Triggers**, all only while the server is running:
  - every N minutes, or every chosen weekday at a fixed time;
  - a local webhook: `POST http://127.0.0.1:3000/hook/<address>` with the brief as plain text or as JSON `{"input": "..."}`. The answer holds a `check` address that returns the run's status and final output. Only programs on this computer can reach it. The address is secret, and you can replace it in the editor;
  - a watched workspace folder: each new or changed text file starts a run with the file in the brief. Files already in the folder when you turn it on are left alone;
  - an email: SILO checks an IMAP inbox every minute and starts a run for each new message, with the sender, subject and text in the brief. You can keep only mail from a sender or with a word in the subject. Mail that was already there is left alone, and messages stay unread. The connection uses TLS on port 993. Gmail and Outlook need an app password;
  - your phone: `/run` in the Telegram bot (see Phone below).
  - something new in an app: a new email in Gmail or Outlook, a new row in a Google Sheet, a new or changed file in Google Drive, a calendar event about to start, a new message in a Slack channel, a new issue or pull request on GitHub, a new or changed page in a Notion database. Without any account: a new item in an RSS or Atom feed, or a web page that changes (optionally only when it contains a word you pick). SILO asks the app every 15 seconds, every minute or every 5 minutes (you pick; feeds and web pages at most once a minute) with the account connected in IT / Uplink, starts one run for each new thing, and puts it in the brief marked as coming from outside. What was there before you turned the trigger on is left alone. A check that fails shows why on the workflow and is tried again a few minutes later.
    - Instant events. GitHub issues and pull requests and Slack messages can also arrive the moment they happen. Tick Instant, save, and open the workflow again for the address to give the app. GitHub signs each event with a secret SILO makes, and SILO can add the webhook to the repository for you with your token (it needs the admin:repo_hook scope or Webhooks write). Slack needs an app of your own: paste its signing secret, and give the address under Event Subscriptions. Events with a wrong signature, or Slack events older than 5 minutes, are refused. The regular checks keep running, and one issue or message never starts the workflow twice. The address has to be reachable from the internet, so this needs remote access turned on.
    - Events from about 40 more apps. Pick "An app sends an event" and the app: Stripe, Shopify, WooCommerce, Square, Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, GitHub (any event), GitLab, Bitbucket, Sentry, PagerDuty, Vercel, Netlify, Jira, Linear, Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Todoist, Notion, Calendly, Typeform, Zoom, Discord, a Telegram bot, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram, Twilio, Mailgun, Resend, Clerk and other apps that sign with Svix, Zendesk, Intercom, HubSpot, Webflow, Dropbox, Postmark and Pipedrive (user and password), and any other app that signs with HMAC SHA-256 or sends a secret in a header. Give the secret the app shows (the editor says where to find it), save, and give the app the address. SILO checks each event the way that app signs it, refuses a wrong signature and, where the app sends the time, anything older than 5 minutes, and answers the app's own address checks (Asana, Notion, Dropbox, Meta, Trello, Zoom, Discord). Asana and Notion send their secret when the webhook is made, so there is nothing to type. You can keep only some events, like "invoice, checkout.session". Each event starts the workflow once.
- **Versions**: every saved change to a workflow keeps the version before it, up to 20. Versions shows them side by side, and Restore brings one back as a new version. Each run records the version it ran.
- **Replay**: a finished run can be replayed with the same brief, from the start or from a chosen step. From a later step, the earlier steps keep the answers of the old run and are marked as such in the trace.
- **Tests**: a workflow and a resident can each keep up to 20 test cases. Open Tests on a workflow, or the check button on a resident's card. A case is a brief (for a resident, a message answered as a new conversation, its chat history neither used nor changed) plus checks on the answer, one per line:
  - `contains:`, `missing:` and `regex:` look at the text;
  - `field: total=1200` compares a field of the result shape (workflows only);
  - `judge: names the supplier and the due date` has a resident score the answer from 0 to 10 against the requirement; it passes at 7;
  - `maxcost: 0.05` (dollars), `maxms: 30` (seconds) and `maxtokens: 5000` are limits for one attempt;
  - `x2` to `x5` after the kind, like `judge x3: ...`, makes a check count more in the score.

  A case can also keep a **reference answer** you approve; the judge gets it to know what a good answer holds. Approval steps pass on their own and app steps that change something are not called in a test run. Test runs and their traces never push out real ones, and they do not sound the alarm.
- **Several runs per case**: each case runs 1 to 5 times (3 by default). A case passes when every run passed and is **flaky** when only some did. Each report has a score from 0 to 100 (the mean of the case scores, weights and judge scores included) and the pass rate over every run with its 95% interval.
- **Case by case against the report before**: every case is marked broke, fixed, worse, better, flaky, new or the same compared with the last report on the same model. A case breaks when it passes clearly less often than before (by half or more), not after one unlucky run. A report **got worse** when a case broke, a case scored clearly lower while passing as often, or the pass rate over every run fell more than chance explains (a one-sided test at 95%). Then there is an event and a notification. **Compare** shows a case's answer next to the one before it, and what changed in between: the steps, the prompts and models of the residents in them.
- **Tests on a change**: in Tests, choose what happens when a resident's prompt, model or tools, or a workflow's brief or steps change:
  - *Nothing happens*;
  - *Run the tests* after the change. A resident's change also runs the tests of every workflow with that resident whose tests run on change.
  - *Hold the change until nothing got worse*. The tested part of the change waits as a **held change**, not in use; the rest (a name, a schedule) goes in at once. Its tests run, after a run of the current version when it has none. When nothing got worse the change goes in by itself. Otherwise it waits for you: What changed shows the difference, and you apply it anyway, reject it or test it again.

  A resident whose tests got worse, or whose change made a workflow worse, shows an orange down arrow in the silo and "Tests got worse" on its card until a later report is fine again.
- **Cases from real work**: a finished run of a workflow, or the last answer of a resident, becomes a case in one click, its answer the reference. With **Learn from rejections** on, a run you reject with a note becomes a judged case on the same brief.
- **A judge you can check**: mark each judged verdict Right or Wrong. Below 80% agreement over 5 or more verdicts the judge is marked unreliable and SILO does not pick it on its own. Without a chosen judge, SILO picks a resident with another model than the one doing the work when there is one, and the report says when the judge shares the model.
- **Compare models**: in Tests, pick up to three models. The same tests run once per model, every step on that model, and a table shows passes, score, cost, time and tokens side by side.
- **From CI**: `SILO_TOKEN=silo_pat_... npm run eval` runs the tests of every workflow and resident that has them (or only those named: `npm run eval -- "Invoice reader" Atlas`), waits, prints each report and exits with 1 when one got worse or did not finish. Options: `--url`, `--repeat 1-5`, `--min-score 0-100`, `--strict` (every case must pass), `--timeout` seconds, `--json`. The token needs the read and run scopes. SILO keeps the last 40 reports per workflow or resident; the answers of cases stay in the newest 8.
- **Result shape**: a workflow can promise fixed fields (text, number, date, yes or no, list), as one object or a list of them. The last resident step is told the fields, and SILO checks the answer at the end of every run: missing fields, numbers that are not numbers, dates that are not dates. "1,200.50" becomes 1200.5 and "no" becomes false. A wrong answer goes back to that resident once for a fix; if it is still wrong, the run fails and says what is wrong. The run output is the clean JSON, and the webhook `check` address returns the fields as `data`.
- **Token budget**: a run stops before its next step once it has used the set number of tokens. Raise the budget and resume the run to finish it.
- **The Order**: the book of procedures of the silo, opened from Flows. Sixteen procedures in five chapters:
  - Knowledge: Research brief, Decision memo, Council session;
  - Making: Code change with review, Article;
  - Standing orders: Inbox triage, Morning report;
  - Office work: Invoice to fields, Meeting notes to tasks, Mail digest (Gmail or Outlook), GitHub issue triage, Weekly report in Word;
  - Apps on their own: Mail into a sheet, Issues into Notion (both with no model), Today in Slack (calendar, a resident writes the agenda, your OK, then Slack), Ask, wait, sum up (a Slack question, two days of waiting, a summary in the channel). Fields like the sheet link or the Slack channel say "Paste ... here" until you fill them in.

  Each page names the residents who would carry out every step, the permissions a step needs and whether that resident has them, the conditions, reviews and approvals, the result shape, and how it starts. Adopt a procedure to open it in the editor, where you can change anything before you save. Test cases that come with it are saved with the workflow.
- **Share and import**: Share on a workflow gives it as a procedure file (`.silo.json`) or keeps it in the Order under Your procedures, for everyone on this SILO.
  - The file holds the steps, their instructions, the name, role and sector of each resident, the result shape, and the tests with their briefs. App steps keep what they fill in, like addresses, channels or sheet links, so read them before you send the file; a connector goes by its name, and the SILO that imports it uses its own connector with that name. It holds no keys, webhook addresses, mail settings, runs or files.
  - Import a procedure, in the Order, reads a file or pasted text and checks it with the same rules as a workflow. Anything else in the file is dropped.
  - You read it as a page of the Order before anything is saved. Residents are matched by name, then by role, then by sector.
  - A procedure from a file never starts on its own and gives no resident a new permission. Its instructions become instructions for your residents, so read them first.
- **Couriers**: every time a step hands work to a resident, a courier carries the folder through the 3D silo: along the gallery, over the bridge, up or down the spiral stair and over to the next resident. The first step comes down from level 1. A faint amber outline keeps the courier visible behind the stair core. Watch the courier, in the run trace, rides the world camera along with it and on to the next courier.
- Each workflow step starts from a clean conversation. It does not see the resident's chat history and is not added to it.
- The trace shows the text of the current step as it is written. Every run is saved with each step's output, tokens, reviews and errors.
- Rate limits, overloaded providers and dropped connections are tried again up to three times. An answer that hits the model's output limit is continued up to two times, and the trace warns if it was still cut off.
- A failed, stopped or interrupted run can be resumed from the step where it ended. The steps that already finished are kept.
- A running workflow can be stopped at once from its trace, even in the middle of a step. Finished runs can be started again with the same brief, and every output can be copied.
- If a step's resident is busy with another mission, the step waits for them instead of failing.
- A workflow can keep a saved brief. Scheduled runs use it, and it is filled in when you run the workflow by hand.
- Workflows can run on a timer while the server is on. A workflow never runs twice at the same time. A trigger that arrives while the workflow is already running, its first resident's floor is on low power, or its run fails waits in a queue of up to 15 and runs once the workflow is free. The workflow's `missedPolicy`, set through the API, decides what is kept: `coalesce` (the default) keeps one copy of a repeated trigger, `queue` keeps every one, `skip` drops them. Pause stops new missions and stops a workflow between steps. Requests already sent still finish.
- Conversations sent to a model leave out failed exchanges and always start with your message, so long chats keep working with every provider.
- Shared text files in `workspace/`.
- Tools, set per resident under Permissions. They work in chats and in workflow steps, with every provider:
  - **Search and read workspace files**: the resident sees the list of files, searches them by words and reads the ones it needs (up to 30 KB each). Nothing is pasted in up front.
  - **Propose files**: a file the resident writes waits in the Archive under "Waiting for your approval". View it (next to the current file if it replaces one), edit it before saving, save it or discard it. The Archive key shows how many are waiting.
  - **Read web pages and search the web**: the resident reads public pages by address, as plain text, and searches with DuckDuckGo (no key), Brave, Tavily or your own SearXNG, chosen in System. Addresses on this computer or your local network are refused, also after redirects.
  - **Use a web browser**: the resident drives a real Edge or Chrome, the one already on this computer, with nothing to install. It opens pages, reads their text with a numbered list of links, buttons and fields, follows links, scrolls and goes back on its own. Pressing a button, typing or choosing an option waits for your OK, and the request names the site, the element and the exact text. Always on a site allows that site only. Each resident has its own browser profile under data/browser, apart from yours and from the others, and it closes after five minutes without use.
    - Sign-ins: residents never type passwords, codes or card numbers, and never type a saved key into a page. On the resident card, Sign in to a site opens a window with that resident's profile. You sign in there and close it, and the resident uses that sign-in from then on. Sign out everywhere deletes the profile.
    - Safety: all the browser's traffic, from every page, frame and worker, goes through a small guard proxy in SILO that looks up each address itself, and every request is also checked over the DevTools protocol. Pages and scripts cannot reach this computer or your network, also when a public name points there. Downloads, pop-up windows and dialogs are blocked. Page text reaches the model marked as untrusted data.
    - Seeing: "look" takes a screenshot and gives it to the model, for maps, charts, canvas pages and layout. It goes only to models that read images: OpenAI and Anthropic models, and others whose name says so (gpt-4o, gemini, llava, qwen-vl and the like). Sees screenshots, in the resident editor, sets it by hand. Only the newest screenshot stays in the conversation.
    - What it did: every action leaves a screenshot in the trace, and the resident card shows the last page it saw. In System you can pick the browser program and show the window while residents work, to watch them live.
  - **Use desktop apps (Windows)**: the resident lists the open windows, reads one as text with a numbered list of its controls (through Windows accessibility, the same thing screen readers use), and can look at a screenshot of it. It presses buttons, fills fields, ticks boxes and picks items through the controls themselves, so your mouse and keyboard stay yours. Only when a control cannot be used that way does it click with the mouse or type keys, and the request tells you so first.
    - Seeing a window and every action wait for your OK, per app. Always on notepad allows that app only. The request names the window, the control and the exact text or keys.
    - Never used: browsers (residents have their own), terminals, shells and code editors, system tools (Settings, Registry, Task Manager, security prompts) and password managers. Passwords are never typed, and neither is a saved key.
    - Every action leaves a screenshot of the window in the trace. Screenshots are taken from the window itself, so it does not have to come to the front.
    - Apps built on modern Windows parts (WPF, WinUI, Office, Explorer, most current apps) show all their controls. Some older apps show fewer, and then the resident uses look and clicks by position.
  - **Search the Archive by meaning**: with an embedding model set in System (OpenAI, Ollama or a compatible endpoint), workspace files are also found by meaning, so "lubricant" finds a note about oil. Without one, search uses keywords.
  - **Run code**: Python, JavaScript or PowerShell, in Docker without network when Docker is installed, otherwise as a local process on a copy of the workspace with a time limit. You approve each run in Signals. Files the code changes come back as proposals. The local mode is not a full sandbox.

## AI on this computer

Chrome and Edge 138 and newer carry small AI models of their own. SILO uses them for three things, inside the browser, without a key, without cost, and without sending the text anywhere:

- **Translate** on every answer in a chat and on every step of a run. The language is found by itself, long answers are translated piece by piece so the first lines show at once, and languages without a direct pair go through English. Code is never translated: blocks between ``` fences stay exactly as they are, and inline code keeps its place in the translated sentence.
- **Key points** on answers longer than a few paragraphs. Text longer than the model takes is summed up in parts, then the parts together. The summary model writes English, Spanish and Japanese; for other languages its key points are translated.
- **Brief me** in Signals sums up what happened in the silo over the last day. Without the summary model it counts what happened and lists the errors and the latest events instead.

Pick the language in System, AI on this computer. There you can also have new answers in another language translated by themselves (only answers to what you send from that chat, not older ones), and download the models with one click. The language packs are small and come down on first use; the summary model is a few gigabytes, needs a recent computer and is only downloaded from that button, never by a click on Key points. Results are kept in this browser, so a reload shows them again until the text they came from changes. They belong to the person signed in: signing out, a session that ends or someone else signing in on the same browser removes them. The choices and results stay in this browser only. Where the browser does not have these models, or does not answer within a few seconds, the buttons stay hidden and nothing else changes.

## Connecting a model

1. Open **UPLINK**.
2. Pick a provider and enter the exact model ID from your account (or an installed Ollama model).
3. Click **Test connection**. Saving a connection does not check it.
4. Open a resident's settings in **RESIDENTS**, choose the provider, optionally set a different model, and save.
5. Send a message or run a workflow.

Keys typed in the app are saved encrypted (AES-256-GCM) in `data/secrets.enc`. The key is in `data/vault.key`, or comes from a passphrase when you start SILO with `SILO_SECRET` set. Keep both files private, and back them up together. You can also set `OPENAI_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` and `COMPATIBLE_API_KEY` before starting the server, then set a model in the app. Keys are never sent to the browser, written to `state.json`, or included in exports. A custom endpoint receives its key and your prompts, so only use one you trust.

Provider requests follow the official docs: [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/quickstart), [Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/messages/create), [Ollama](https://docs.ollama.com/api/chat). What works and what it costs depends on your model and account. Live provider calls were not tested here, because that needs your keys.

## API for programs

Scripts, CI jobs and other servers use the same API as the page, with a personal token instead of a cookie. Make one in System > API tokens. It is shown once; SILO keeps only its SHA-256.

- A token acts as the person who made it and never has more rights than their role, checked again on every request. When the person is removed, their tokens go too. Tokens made while there are no accounts stop working when the first account is made.
- Scopes: `read` (everything you can GET), `run` (talk to residents, run workflows and tests, change residents, workflows and files), `approve` (tool requests, run approvals, proposed files, held changes) and `admin` (owner settings). A viewer can only give `read`, an operator `read`, `run` and `approve`.
- A token never signs in, changes a password, or manages accounts, tokens, single sign-on or the key vault.
- Tokens work from this computer only, unless made to work from anywhere (network and remote address). They expire after 7, 30, 90 or 365 days, or never. At most 300 requests a minute per token. Revoking one also closes its live streams. The audit log names the token behind each change.
- The API is described in OpenAPI 3.1 at `/api/openapi.json`, with the scope each call needs.
- Tests: `POST /api/workflows/{id}/evaluate` or `/api/agents/{id}/evaluate` starts them and returns the report; `GET /api/evals/{id}` follows it, `GET /api/evals/{id}/diff` compares it with the one before. `npm run eval` does all of that for CI.

```sh
curl -H "Authorization: Bearer $SILO_TOKEN" http://127.0.0.1:3000/api/state
```

```python
import os, requests
silo = requests.Session()
silo.headers["Authorization"] = "Bearer " + os.environ["SILO_TOKEN"]
base = "http://127.0.0.1:3000/api"
state = silo.get(base + "/state").json()
atlas = next(a for a in state["agents"] if a["name"] == "Atlas")
print(silo.post(f"{base}/agents/{atlas['id']}/chat", json={"message": "Summarize today's runs."}).json()["text"])
run = silo.post(f"{base}/workflows/{state['workflows'][0]['id']}/run", json={"input": "Weekly report"}).json()
```

### Python SDK

`sdk/python` is the `silo-os` package: only the standard library, Python 3.9 or newer, typed, with `Silo` and `AsyncSilo`. It waits and tries again after 429 as long as `Retry-After` says, raises `AuthError`, `PermissionDenied`, `NotFound`, `Conflict`, `RateLimited` and friends, and sends the token only over https or to this computer. `silo eval`, `silo ask` and `silo run` work from a terminal or CI. See [sdk/python/README.md](sdk/python/README.md); build it with `python -m build sdk/python`.

```python
from silo_os import Silo
silo = Silo()                                    # SILO_URL and SILO_TOKEN
print(silo.ask("Atlas", "Summarize today's runs.").text)
run = silo.run("Weekly report", "This week")     # waits for the run
assert not silo.evaluate("Weekly report")["worse"]
```

## SILO for outside programs

An owner turns these on in System > Outside programs and picks the residents and workflows they may use. Nothing is exposed before that. Callers use an API token: its scopes and its person's role decide every call, residents still ask you before tools that need your OK, and the audit log names the token. Questions from outside start a new conversation and leave the resident's chat alone.

- **MCP server** at `/api/serve/mcp` (Streamable HTTP, JSON answers, protocol 2025-06-18, 2025-03-26 and 2024-11-05). Claude Desktop, Claude Code, Cursor, LangGraph, CrewAI and other MCP clients get `ask_<resident>` and `run_<workflow>` (scope run; a run waits up to 50 seconds, then gives its id), `silo_get_run` and `silo_pending` (read), `silo_decide` (approve), `silo_run_tests` (run) and `silo_test_report` (read). A token only sees the tools its scopes allow. System shows configs to copy; they never contain a token.
- **A2A agent** (Agent2Agent 0.3): the card is at `/.well-known/agent-card.json` (and `/agent.json`) while the agent is on, and it is public, as A2A cards are: it names what you exposed. JSON-RPC at `/api/serve/a2a`: `message/send` (blocking by default, up to two minutes), `message/stream` and `tasks/resubscribe` (server-sent events), `tasks/get` and `tasks/cancel`. Each skill is a resident or a workflow; a message without `metadata.skill` goes to the only one exposed or to the resident you pick. Text and data parts are accepted, not files. A follow-up with the same `contextId` lets a resident see the earlier turns. Tasks belong to the token that made them and stay in memory for a day (at most 300); push notifications are not supported.
- **Outside agents** (A2A client): add another agent by its address in System; SILO reads its card and keeps its token in the vault. The card must send tasks to its own origin, so it cannot redirect the token. Residents with `delegate` use `ask_agent`; the first task to each agent waits for your OK (allow always trusts that agent for that resident), and its answer is marked untrusted.

## Notebooks

Residents with `run_code` also get `notebook_run`: a persistent Python kernel per resident and notebook, like Jupyter but without needing Jupyter. Variables, imports and data stay between cells; the value of the last line is shown; matplotlib figures are kept as PNG. Each cell is approved like `run_code` (the exact code by its SHA-256, and where it runs: Docker without network when available, otherwise a local process on a copy of the workspace). A cell has 120 seconds (`SILO_NOTEBOOK_TIMEOUT` in ms); a cell over time stops the kernel. Files a cell writes become proposals. A kernel stops after 15 minutes without use (at most four run at once) or with `notebook_reset`. Notebooks are saved as `.ipynb` (nbformat 4.5) under `data/notebooks`; System shows them and can restart a kernel, save a copy to the Archive or delete them.

## Silo-to-Silo Radio

Residents can talk to residents of other SILO instances. Owners set it up in System > Silo-to-Silo Radio.

- Every silo makes its own Ed25519 key pair on its first start. The private half stays encrypted in the vault; the public key and its fingerprint are shown in System, and served at `/radio/identity` while the radio is on.
- To add another silo, write its address and press Read key. Before you save, ask its owner to read their fingerprint to you, by phone or in person, and compare. Then they add yours the same way. No secret is shared.
- Every transmission is signed with the sender's key and names the key it is for. Answers are signed by the receiver and carry the nonce of the question. The receiver knows the sender by the key that checks out, never by the name in the message.
- Addresses use https. Plain http works only on this computer and the local network (like `http://192.168.1.20:3000` or `http://silo.local:3000`); every question and answer is signed, so it cannot be forged on the way, but it is not encrypted. An address written without `http://` gets http on the local network and https elsewhere. The radio switch, this silo's name and its address are saved one at a time, as soon as you change them.
- Per silo: whether this silo sends to it, whether it takes transmissions from it, which residents it may reach (empty means all), messages an hour (default 30) and tokens a day (0 means no limit).
- Without a capability, a resident answers radio questions from what it knows, with no tools.
- The resident is found by exact name, then exact role. Nothing partial and no fallback. Requests older than five minutes and repeated nonces are refused, and inbound messages are marked untrusted.
- Residents with the `delegate` permission use the `radio_silo` tool, naming the silo when more than one is connected.
- An older SILO that only knows radio version 2 can still be added with a shared key (marked "old shared key"). Update both silos to use keys.

### Capabilities between silos

Under Give rights, an owner gives another silo the right to use some residents with some tools (`read_files`, `write_files`, `web`) in some folders, for some hours, with a budget of tokens and messages. For example: read only `reports/`, 2 hours, 50,000 tokens.

- The result is a signed token (`silocap1.…`) bound to the key of the silo it is for. No other silo can use it, and a change breaks the signature. Send it to the other owner, who adds it under Rights held.
- The silo that holds rights can pass on part of them (Pass on). Each link of the chain is signed by the silo that gives it and may only be narrower: fewer residents, tools and folders, a smaller budget, an earlier end. A third silo that the owner of the data has never met can then use exactly what it was passed, nothing more.
- Everything is checked where the data lives, with no central server: the chain starts with that silo's own key, each link narrows the one before, the last link names the key that signed the request, and no link is revoked or expired.
- The resident gets only those tools, file operations and folders, even if a local grant for it allows more.
- Budgets are counted at the silo that owns the data, for every link of the chain. A silo pays for what it passed on, too.
- Revoke stops a capability at once. A capability that was passed on is revoked at the silo that owns the data, which checks that the revocation is signed by the silo that signed that link. Revoking a link stops everything passed on from it.
- On the air lists what went over the radio: when, which silo, which resident, whether a capability was used, tokens, and why something was refused. It never keeps the messages.
- The map in System shows this silo in the middle and the others around it: the radio links (lit when used in the last ten minutes), the capabilities with a bar of the budget left, and the messages of the last minute and a half. On a phone it is a list.

## Data and limits

- `data/state.db` (SQLite) holds residents, conversations, workflows, runs, traces and the activity log. Each save writes only what changed, in one transaction, so a crash leaves the last complete save. Every row has a checksum, and a damaged database is moved aside and rebuilt from the copies below.
- `data/state.json` is a readable copy, written every five minutes when something changed and when the server stops. `data/state.json.bak` is the copy before it, and SQLite also keeps three hourly snapshots. To go back to a copy, stop the server and put it in place as `data/state.json`: on the next start a `state.json` changed by hand is loaded instead of the database.
- `workspace/` holds the shared files. They stay on this computer unless a resident with read access sends them to an external provider.
- Text files residents can read: txt, md, json, csv, js, ts, html, css, yaml, yml, up to 30 KB per file. Documents (pdf, docx, xlsx, xlsm, pptx, odt, ods, odp) up to 15 MB, of which the first 40,000 characters of text are read. Files you add in the Archive can be up to 25 MB. The file list shows at most 150 files. Web pages are cut at 20,000 characters. The last 20 messages are sent as chat context, and the last 100 are kept per resident.
- `data/secrets.enc` and `data/vault.key` hold your keys, tokens and passwords for connectors and channels, encrypted.
- Only relative file paths are accepted. Paths that leave the folder, alternate data streams, reserved Windows names and symbolic links are refused. The server checks the Host header, needs a session cookie, and needs the same Origin plus a CSRF token for every change. If the server restarts, the page gets a new session on its own.
- Exports contain your conversations and history. Treat them as private.
- The page asks for the state every two seconds, but the server only sends it again when something changed. Otherwise the answer is empty.
- The registry shows 48 residents at a time. Show more loads the next 48, and searching or picking a sector starts over.

## People and network access

With no accounts SILO answers only on this computer and asks nobody to sign in. In System, create an owner account to turn on sign-in. Owners can add more people as:

- **Owner**: everything, including keys, connectors, channels, spending, settings and people.
- **Operator**: talks to residents, runs and edits workflows, approves requests. Cannot change keys, connectors, channels, spending, settings or people. Runs app steps but cannot add, change or move one, nor bring one back from an older version, because an app step uses your accounts and connectors directly; the editor shows them locked. Removing one is allowed.
- **Viewer**: looks at everything and changes nothing.

Sign-in happens on a green terminal floating in the shaft of the silo. Behind it, in phosphor green, the camera falls down the shaft past eight-sided floors with lit windows, pipes, a spiral stair and bridges, then slows to a drift while the view turns and steam moves. The terminal comes out of the dark and leans toward the pointer, the title resolves out of random signs, and the Warden wakes and asks for your name and password. Any key or click finishes the opening at once and lands in the next field. A wrong password turns the shaft amber and sounds the alarm; the right one sends the camera diving down the shaft into the silo. With reduced motion turned on in your system, it all shows at once without animation.

Passwords are hashed with scrypt. Ten wrong passwords from one address lock it out for ten minutes. An owner can open SILO to other devices on the network. It takes effect after a restart, and the server prints the addresses to use. Traffic is plain HTTP, so only do this on a network you trust, or put a TLS proxy in front. If you lose every owner password, stop the server and remove the `users` list from `data/state.json`.

### Open from anywhere

System, Open from anywhere, gives SILO a secure HTTPS address through a Cloudflare Tunnel, for your phone or any other network. SILO keeps listening only on this computer; cloudflared makes an outgoing connection to Cloudflare and brings the requests back. Nothing is opened on your router and no firewall rule is needed.

- It needs an owner account, and everyone signs in. Over the tunnel the session cookie is HTTPS only, the browser is told to stay on HTTPS, and the page only accepts requests from the tunnel address.
- Ten wrong passwords from one internet address lock that address for ten minutes, and thirty wrong passwords from the whole internet lock all sign-ins over the tunnel for ten minutes. Signing in on this computer keeps working.
- One-time links from Windows notifications only work on this computer.
- **Quick address**: free and without an account, a random `https://....trycloudflare.com` address that changes each time SILO starts. When the phone bot is connected, it sends you the new address.
- **Your own domain**: create a tunnel in the Cloudflare dashboard (Networks, Tunnels), paste its token, and add a public hostname with service `http://127.0.0.1:3000`. The address stays the same. The token is kept in the encrypted vault and given to cloudflared through its environment, not its command line.
- cloudflared is found in `data/bin`, Program Files, WinGet or PATH. The Download button gets it from Cloudflare's GitHub releases and keeps it only if it carries a valid signature from Cloudflare. You can also run `winget install --id Cloudflare.cloudflared`.

### Single sign-on

In System, Single sign-on, people sign in with your company account through OpenID Connect: Microsoft Entra ID, Google Workspace, Okta, Auth0, Keycloak or any provider with discovery. Register SILO in your provider as a web app, with the redirect address the card shows (`http://127.0.0.1:3000/sso/callback`, or your tunnel address), then give SILO the issuer, the client ID and the secret. It needs an owner account first.

- SILO uses the authorization code flow with PKCE, a nonce, and a state tied to the browser that started the sign-in. It checks the id_token itself: the signature against the provider's published keys, the issuer, the audience, the time and the nonce.
- The account is made at the first sign-in. You choose which email domains get in, and what role new people get: none, viewer or operator.
- Group rules give roles from a claim (`groups`, `roles`, or a path like `realm_access.roles`). With rules, the role follows the provider at every sign-in, and the last owner always stays owner.
- An owner who removes someone keeps them out, until the owner lets removed people in again.
- Names and passwords can stay on everywhere, or only work on the SILO computer as a way back in.

### Audit log

Every sign-in (also the failed ones), every change made through SILO, every decision from the page, a phone or a Windows notification, every webhook start and every tool a resident uses goes into `data/audit`, one file per month. Each record says who, when, from which address and how it went. Passwords, keys and message text are never written into it.

Each record holds an HMAC of the one before it, made with a key from the encrypted vault. Check the chain in System, Audit log: a record that is changed, removed or slipped in shows up, with the file and line. Export a date range as CSV (for Excel) or JSON lines (for a SIEM). You choose how long records are kept: always, or 1 to 7 years.

### OpenTelemetry

In System, OpenTelemetry, SILO sends traces to your own collector over OTLP/HTTP with JSON: the OpenTelemetry Collector, Grafana, Honeycomb, Datadog, Jaeger, Langfuse and others. Every conversation, workflow run and goal is a trace; model calls, tool uses, questions to other residents and approvals are spans, with the GenAI conventions (`gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens` and so on) plus cost. The audit log can go along as logs. Prompts and answers are never sent; tool arguments only when you turn that on, and without secrets. Headers, such as an API key for the collector, are kept in the vault. Plain http is only allowed to this computer or your own network. When the collector is away, SILO keeps up to 5,000 spans and tries again, waiting up to five minutes.

### Speed on slow connections

Answers are compressed with Brotli or gzip. Files are checked with an ETag, so a reload sends only what changed, and the 3D library is cached for a week. SILO can be added to the home screen of a phone as an app.

### Speed in the page

- A live channel (server-sent events, compressed and flushed event by event) sends the silo state once, then only what changed: one resident that starts working is a patch of a few hundred bytes instead of the whole state, and it arrives within about 150 ms instead of on the next two second check. The text a resident is writing comes the same way, a few letters at a time. When the channel is down, the page goes back to checking every two seconds.
- Every script is asked for at once (module preload), so the page is ready in well under 100 ms on this computer instead of over a second.
- The artwork loads as WebP (about 0.7 MB instead of 3.5 MB), with the PNG as a fallback.
- The shaders compile in the background where the graphics driver allows it, and the 432 residents are painted by workers off the main thread (OffscreenCanvas), so the world appears without a half second stall.
- The 3D picture adapts its sharpness to the device in both directions: it starts sharp, steps down when frames take too long and back up when there is room, up to the screen's own resolution (1.5 on phones).
- Shadows are drawn again only when they would come out different: when the camera changes level, levels come into view or the machines at the bottom turn. Elsewhere the last shadow map is reused, which saves about a sixth of the draw calls in each frame with exactly the same picture.
- Moving between terminals cross-fades (View Transitions) and dialogs fade out, unless the system asks for reduced motion. Settings cards further down the page are only laid out when they scroll into view.

## Limits

SILO runs on your own computer. It is not a hosted service or a Windows service: it runs while you are signed in, and starts with you when start at sign-in is on. It does not make voice or video calls. The phone bot and email triggers only work while the server runs. Local code runs are not a full sandbox, so keep approval on, or install Docker. Scheduling stops when the server stops. When it starts again, a workflow whose time passed runs once, however many runs were missed, whatever its `missedPolicy`.
